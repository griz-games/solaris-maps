# Spy v. Spy

**36 players · 9 galaxies · 36 wormholes**

*Someone is always watching you.*

Go back to your Solaris salad days: a new player galaxy and only 4 players. But you'll need to grow up fast. The galaxy is bigger than you think.

Nine self-contained galaxies sit on a ring. Four players share each galaxy. 36 wormholes, one for every
galaxy pair, and exactly one end of every wormhole is a **neutral black hole spy post**, sitting where nobody can sail to it. Every post hangs at the end of one player's own lane and watches that player and nobody else. There's nowhere for you to hide so you better move fast.

This is a game of local battles and long-distance intelligence. Will you fight off your starting neighbors to claim the juicy core at the center? Or will you play nice and plan out a broader alliance to face all the other threats?

Your starting position has a galactic core that is worth more than everything else around it. Your most valuable information is about someone four galaxies away who you cannot reach and cannot hurt you — yet. How will you proceed?

## Map features

### Spy posts

The map consists of 9 galaxies, connected by **36 distinct worm hole pairs**. No two galaxies are linked twice, and no galaxy links to itself.
Every galaxy therefore has 8 wormhole stars, one reaching each of the other eight.

So for every player there are:

- **A spy post**, a neutral black hole carrying a telescope array. Its nearest neighbour of any kind is 4 hy away, so the only way onto it is through its wormhole.

  Every post sits directly behind one player, on that player's own bisector, past their nebula and their
  gateway. Take it and you are looking down exactly one throat: their **capital**, the two satellites on
  their outward frontier, their nebula and their gateway — three of that player's six opening stars, live,
  and no part of anybody else's position. The nearest star belonging to any *other* player is more than
  twice the post's scan range away.

- **A gateway**, swung 19 degrees off that player's bisector so it sits *beside* their pod rather than at
  the end of it — level with their outer satellites, one hyperspace-1 hop off the edge of their position.
  It is 199u from their capital, out of reach on the hyperspace 2 everyone starts with, so it is never a
  free opening asset; but their pod can walk to it at hyperspace 1 in 12 ticks.

  That swing is the only thing on the map without a mirror image. Each galaxy is a **pinwheel**: a quarter
  turn maps it exactly onto itself — so all 36 players occupy the same position, turned — but a reflection
  maps everything *except* the gateways. Nothing that decides fairness rides on it: the contested binaries
  are still equidistant from both their neighbours to within a millionth of a unit.

### Resource escalation

Natural resources are not scattered randomly. 

Stars get increasingly tempting as you move towards the center: 10 at the fringe, a 25 average, and 125 on the pulsar at the exact middle — a disproportionately valuable star that only one player can own, and it starts with 10/10/5 infrastructure already on it. It is exactly 39 ticks from every one of the 36 starting positions, so nobody gets a head start.

### What hyperspace buys you

Nothing on this map is gated behind research. On the hyperspace 1 you open with you can already walk to your
own wormhole (12 ticks) and to the galactic core (39). What the next level buys is a **shorter road, not a
longer jump**.

The inner ring of eight stars sits 173u out from the core: too far for a hyperspace-1 carrier to drop
straight in, one clean hop for a hyperspace-2 one. So at hyperspace 1 the run to the core has to swing out
around a pair of bridge stars set off to either side, and pays **3 ticks** for the detour — 39 against 36.
Every player faces exactly the same trade, on exactly the same geometry.
